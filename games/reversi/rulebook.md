# Othello (Reversi) — Official Rules

## Object of the game

Othello is a two-player strategy game played on an 8×8 board using discs
that are black on one side and white on the other. One player plays the
black side of the discs, the other the white side. The object of the game
is to have the majority of discs showing your colour on the board at the
end of the game.

## Equipment

- A board of 64 squares, arranged in 8 rows and 8 columns. Columns are
  labelled **a** to **h** from left to right; rows are labelled **1** to
  **8** from top to bottom. A square is named by its column and row, e.g.
  **d3**.
- 64 identical discs, black on one face and white on the other.

## Setup

The game begins with four discs placed in the centre of the board:

- White discs on **d4** and **e5**
- Black discs on **e4** and **d5**

Black always moves first.

## Play

Players take turns making one move per turn.

A move consists of placing one disc of your own colour on an **empty**
square so that it "outflanks" one or more of your opponent's discs.
A disc or an unbroken row of discs is outflanked when it is bordered at
each end by a disc of the opposing colour. Rows may run horizontally,
vertically, or diagonally.

When you outflank one or more rows of opposing discs, every outflanked
disc is turned over (flipped) to show your colour. All outflanked discs in
all directions must be flipped in the same move. Discs are only flipped as
the direct result of a move: a disc that is outflanked "in passing" —
that is, a row that becomes bordered at both ends as a side effect of
flips elsewhere — is **not** flipped.

A move is legal only if it flips at least one opposing disc. You may not
place a disc that flips nothing, and you may not place a disc on an
occupied square.

## Passing

If a player cannot make a legal move, their turn is forfeited and the
opponent moves again. A player may not forfeit their turn voluntarily —
if you have at least one legal move available, you must make one.

## End of the game

The game ends when neither player is able to make a legal move. This most
commonly happens when all 64 squares are occupied, but the game can end
earlier if neither player can flip any disc.

When the game is over, the discs of each colour are counted. The player
with the majority of discs showing their colour wins. If both players
have the same number of discs, the game is a draw.

The winner's score is customarily recorded as the number of discs of
their colour; unclaimed (empty) squares are awarded to the winner when
recording tournament scores.

## Notes for beginners

- It is possible for a game to end with one player having no discs on the
  board at all; that player loses.
- Corner squares, once taken, can never be flipped, which makes them
  strategically valuable.
